## 1. Question presented

The founder is an F-1 secondary-school student who expects to return to Ohio in late August 2026. She is the sole founder and intended 100% owner of Moral Trade, a public-interest coordination platform. She wants to preserve ownership while avoiding unauthorized employment.

Please define, as concretely as possible, which activities she may perform while physically present in the United States, which require employment authorization, and which should be delegated to a genuine manager outside the United States.

The requested opinion should distinguish:

1. passive ownership and investment;
2. member-level governance of a manager-managed LLC;
3. one-time formation, banking, tax, and compliance acts;
4. routine operational services;
5. uncompensated or volunteer services;
6. instructions or supervision given to a foreign manager;
7. work performed while the founder is physically outside the United States; and
8. any activity that could be authorized under the founder's present or a future immigration status.

## 4. Governing official guidance identified for counsel

The following is the present source record; counsel should verify the current law and any controlling agency guidance before relying on it.

1. DHS Study in the States states that F-1 entrepreneurship is employment by “starting and operating your own business,” and says it is available when the work relates to the program of study and the student has approved pre- or post-completion OPT.
2. USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 2, Part F, Chapter 5 describes CPT and OPT as practical training directly related to the major area of study and says practical training may be authorized for an F-1 student at an SEVP-certified college, university, conservatory, or seminary after the applicable full-academic-year requirement.
3. USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 2, Part F, Chapter 6 states that off-campus employment requires authorization and that an F-1 student must receive the required authorization before beginning off-campus work.
4. ICE's SEVIS employment guidance states that unauthorized employment is a status violation and directs DSOs to terminate a SEVIS record if they know a student is engaged in unauthorized employment. It also limits ordinary on-campus employment to qualifying school or school-affiliated work.

These sources strongly support treating routine operation of the founder's own company as employment. They do not, by themselves, provide a sufficiently precise line between passive ownership, occasional member governance, and active operation. That is the core question for counsel.

## 6. Specific questions for written advice

1. Does mere formation and 100% ownership of a manager-managed LLC violate F-1 status if the founder performs no routine services in the United States?
2. Which member-reserved powers may be exercised from Ohio, and how often, without becoming active operation?
3. May the founder sign initial formation, EIN, beneficial-owner, bank, tax, and compliance documents from Ohio? Which acts should instead be completed before U.S. entry or by the manager?
4. Does opening an entity bank account differ legally from administering it on an ongoing basis?
5. Does uncompensated coding, product work, outreach, or management count as employment where the work benefits a founder-owned business?
6. Does directing, supervising, or giving detailed instructions to an overseas manager from Ohio count as employment, even when the manager and all execution are abroad?
7. What level of periodic owner oversight is permissible: monthly reports, annual budget approval, manager evaluation, reserved-matter votes, or emergency intervention?
8. Can the founder protect existing intellectual property, enforce ownership rights, or approve an extraordinary asset transfer without employment authorization?
9. Does the founder's secondary-school F-1 program make CPT and OPT unavailable under the present Form I-20? Is any other work-authorization category realistically available before college?
10. Could qualifying on-campus employment, severe-economic-hardship authorization, an international-organization authorization, or another category ever cover work for Moral Trade on these facts?
11. What records should the LLC and founder maintain to demonstrate passive ownership, foreign management, and the location of services?
12. Would compensation, no compensation, deferred compensation, equity appreciation, or distributions change the analysis?
13. What should the founder disclose to the DSO, and should counsel communicate directly with the DSO?
14. What governance provisions, communication rules, or practical changes are necessary to make delegation genuine rather than nominal?
15. What conduct presents enough risk that it should stop before or immediately upon the founder's return to Ohio?

## 7. Proposed conservative interim boundary

Until counsel gives written advice, the project proposes the following interim rule:

- The founder may retain ownership, receive reports, contribute capital, and act only on genuinely extraordinary member-reserved matters.
- The founder will not code, deploy, conduct product management, recruit users, supervise contractors, negotiate ordinary provider arrangements, handle support, administer the bank account, or decide individual payment or dispute matters while physically in the United States.
- A China-based manager will have actual authority over ordinary operations and will not function as a nominal signatory carrying out continuous founder instructions.
- The founder will document the dates and locations of any services performed outside the United States.
- Any ambiguous activity will be paused and submitted to counsel before performance.

Counsel should replace this interim rule with a precise, workable matrix.
