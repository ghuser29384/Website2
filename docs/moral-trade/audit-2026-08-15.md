# Moral Trade product audit — 2026-08-15

## Status and evidence boundary

This audit is anchored to `main` at `79ca382c3bdc325dfc5a28e2cbbafc1b95640386` and to the current public Moral Trade routes reviewed on 2026-08-15.

The conceptual sources are:

- Toby Ord, “Moral Trade” (2015).
- William MacAskill and Fin Moorhouse, “Convergence and Compromise” (2025).
- Tom Davidson, William MacAskill, and Mia Taylor, “Moral public goods are a big deal for whether we get a good future” (2026).

The strongest executable product evidence reviewed is draft PR #687 and its isolated-QA evaluator artifact from workflow run `31817890703`, evaluator job `94823848773`, artifact `9225856375`, SHA-256 `46250a09536ba7f2b776d5ab5af9575d7145a16dd7ed07f0426095201fab9a03`.

That artifact proves one synthetic, zero-dollar bilateral path in an isolated QA database: public discovery, private response, atomic selection, frozen terms, bilateral confirmation, private evidence, independent review, resulting state, outsider denial, unilateral exit, and complete fixture cleanup. It does **not** prove production reliability, user demand, causal additionality, charitable impact, payment readiness, or legal readiness.

Moral Trade still has no body of revealed-preference transaction data sufficient to estimate demand, completion, additionality, displacement, or welfare effects. Synthetic tests and conceptual forecasts must not be described as empirical validation.

## Executive judgment

1. **The conceptual and safety architecture is unusually strong.** The product treats the no-trade baseline, action evidence, counterfactual confidence, third-party effects, threats, privacy, exit, appeal, and unsupported impact claims as separate objects rather than collapsing them into a single “trust score.” Confidence: **95%**.
2. **The main product risk is scope exceeding proof.** The repository contains many mechanisms, research programs, workflow variants, and draft release stacks, while only one bounded bilateral zero-dollar loop currently has strong end-to-end isolated-QA evidence. Confidence: **95%**.
3. **The next product objective should be repeated, comprehensible use of one core bilateral loop—not another feature family.** The relevant chain is `understand → inspect terms → respond → freeze → perform → evidence → review → complete or exit`. Confidence: **92%**.
4. **Money movement and provider-confirmed impact credit must remain release-blocked.** Open issues #708, #709, and #337 show that provider authentication, refund reversibility, brand review, destination/legal classification, and live settlement are not complete. Confidence: **99%**.
5. **Moral-public-goods mechanisms should be presented as mechanism experiments, not as a solved free-rider problem.** The cited 2026 paper explicitly finds assurance contracts brittle across important parameter ranges and remains uncertain whether a robust voluntary mechanism exists. Confidence: **97%**.

## Product definition that should govern scope

Moral Trade should be operationally defined as:

> A service for voluntary, reversible, non-coercive coordination that seeks Pareto improvements relative to an explicit no-trade baseline, with scoped evidence, additionality uncertainty, privacy, exit, appeal, and third-party-harm review.

This definition is narrower than “a universal moral coordination platform.” The narrower definition is more defensible and makes every product object testable.

## Theory-to-product requirements

### 1. Moral trade requires a default and participant-relative improvement

Ord’s framework evaluates candidate arrangements relative to the no-trade default and asks whether each participant considers the arrangement better by their own lights. Product consequence: every agreement needs a frozen baseline, explicit participant consent, and a versioned statement of what each side gives and receives.

Current assessment: strong. The bilateral workflow, baseline objects, frozen versions, and two-sided confirmation are correctly central.

### 2. Factual trust and counterfactual trust are different

Evidence that an action occurred does not prove it would not have happened anyway. Product consequence: action evidence, baseline evidence, and additionality confidence must remain separate; a receipt must never automatically become causal-impact credit.

Current assessment: strong in the specification and review language; empirically uncalibrated. The product should continue to abstain when counterfactual evidence is weak.

### 3. Perverse incentives and threats can reverse the value of trade

Ord identifies incentives to manufacture a worse baseline. MacAskill and Moorhouse emphasize that value-destroying threats and extortion can erase much of the gain from trade, especially for downside-sensitive views. Product consequence: no reward for newly escalated harm, cooling-off periods, pre-existing-baseline evidence, threat screening, and refusal to broker “pay me or I harm what you value.”

Current assessment: strong policy design. The anti-threat boundary should remain a release blocker rather than an informational disclaimer.

### 4. Bilateral gains can impose third-party moral externalities

A trade that benefits both participants can still harm people or values not represented in the bargain. Product consequence: affected-party standing, externality review, challenge and appeal routes, and refusal of non-consensual or rights-violating actions.

Current assessment: strong in the public primer and safety surfaces. The next empirical task is to test whether ordinary users understand and use these routes.

### 5. Moral public goods create a genuine free-rider problem

The 2026 moral-public-goods paper shows why many agents may collectively prefer funding a shared moral good while each individually prefers that others pay. It also shows that conclusions depend materially on preference structure and coordination institutions. Product consequence: threshold pools and dominant assurance contracts should expose their assumptions, failure modes, and funding source; they should not claim that dominance bonuses generally solve free-riding.

Current assessment: conceptually serious, but ahead of demonstrated demand and institutional capacity. Keep these mechanisms experimental and non-custodial until the bilateral loop produces reliable user evidence.

## What is already strong

- The public primer explicitly distinguishes moral trade from moral authority, escrow, legal promises, autonomous matching, and threat markets.
- Baseline confidence is separated from proof of action.
- Evidence is scoped to claims and is private by default in the core workflow.
- Exit, challenge, appeal, and third-party recourse are first-class paths.
- The evaluator-facing isolated-QA loop demonstrates immutable terms, private evidence, outsider denial, and zero money movement.
- Repository instructions correctly prohibit fabricated personalization, counts, outcomes, social proof, and unsupported impact claims.

These are substantive advantages. They should be made more visible through a smaller product surface rather than diluted by additional mechanisms.

## Principal findings and required action

### P0 — Keep all money rails disabled until provider and reversal boundaries are complete

Do not merge or activate a donation/payment candidate merely because checkout or webhook happy paths work. Require, at minimum:

- separate public Donate Link and private inbound webhook credentials;
- authentication before request-body parsing or database access;
- idempotent replay and cross-charge rejection;
- append-only provider-refund reversal and net impact accounting;
- exact destination, legal, charitable-solicitation, receipt, privacy, and operator ownership;
- provider brand approval for the exact public presentation;
- protected staging evidence and cleanup tied to the immutable release SHA.

### P0 — Convert isolated core-loop proof into a release-quality, non-conflicting gate

PR #687’s evaluator job is strong evidence, but the enclosing workflow run is not fully green because a separate release-gate browser job failed. Before using it as release evidence:

1. identify and repair or explicitly separate the unrelated failing job;
2. preserve the evaluator artifact and exact-head identity;
3. rerun the complete applicable gate on the final integration candidate;
4. inspect the rendered desktop and mobile screenshots manually;
5. keep production and real participant data outside the test boundary.

### P1 — Make the actual exchange understandable in the first viewport

The current offer record gives substantial first-viewport space to the title and public-record metadata, while the action/request, timing, verification, and failure terms begin lower on the page. The narrow-mobile QA screenshot makes this especially costly: users must traverse a credibility banner, global navigation, a large title, metadata, and action buttons before reaching the commitment preview.

The bounded correction in this branch:

- shortens and structures the contextual-credibility banner;
- adds direct shortcuts to credibility safeguards and the commitment terms;
- reduces offer-hero height and title dominance on backed offer records only;
- stacks actions cleanly on narrow screens;
- preserves every term and public-record field rather than hiding information.

This is a comprehension correction, not a redesign or workflow change.

### P1 — Consolidate routing and retire the legacy root shell

Current `main` routes first human visits through `/walkthrough`, rewrites returning `/` traffic to the compressed legacy `public/moral-trade-live.html` shell, redirects `/offers` to Discover, and rewrites `/create` to `/trades/new`. Draft PR #698 moves activation routing and Walkthrough ownership into the App Router, which is the right direction.

Do not make `src/components/home/home-page.tsx` live in its current illustrative form: it contains fixed names, completion counts, reliability percentages, expiry dates, and match counts that are not backed user data. Keep root routing fail-closed to backed Feed/Discover states or replace every illustrative value with explicit demo labeling.

### P1 — Reduce open-PR and mechanism entropy

The large number of overlapping draft, stacked, historical, shadow, and temporary pull requests makes it difficult to answer a basic question: “What is the current product?” Create one integration ledger that records, for every open PR:

- owning issue and product objective;
- base/head SHA and whether it is stacked;
- runtime, schema, route, and migration overlap;
- release disposition;
- whether it is superseded, blocked, or eligible for integration;
- the one next decision required.

Close or archive superseded temporary work after preserving evidence. Do not merge several individually green feature branches into a product with no integrated user journey.

### P1 — Freeze new feature families and measure the bilateral loop

Until a real pilot cohort exists, freeze new mechanism families. Instrument:

- time from landing to correctly identifying both sides of an offer;
- offer view → response;
- response → mutually frozen agreement;
- agreement → first submitted evidence;
- evidence → reviewed result;
- completion, permissible exit, dispute, and abandonment rates;
- baseline-confidence distribution;
- proportion of actions independently judged additional;
- third-party challenge and upheld-challenge rates;
- participant-reported value on each side, recorded separately;
- safety incidents and near misses.

Page count, route count, synthetic fixture count, and model complexity are not primary product-success metrics.

### P2 — Treat impact estimates as forecasts until calibrated on real behavior

The current modeling work can support scenario planning, not field claims. Future reports and site copy must distinguish:

- participant-stated value;
- verified performance;
- counterfactual additionality;
- redirected versus new resources;
- donation displacement;
- gross versus net provider-confirmed amounts;
- model-implied expected impact;
- empirically observed outcomes.

Do not collapse these into a single “impact” number.

### P2 — Keep technical specification proportional to the user journey

The technical specification is valuable for validators, but the public product should not require ordinary users to understand the entire protocol to complete a safe trade. Maintain a layered model:

1. one-screen exchange summary;
2. exact frozen terms and evidence rules;
3. optional safeguards and recourse;
4. full technical specification for reviewers and researchers.
